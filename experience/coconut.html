The Freshness Dilemma

Building a Global Cold-Chain Supply Network for Thai Coconut Water

Network Design · Sourcing · Scenario Optimization · Tableau

The business problem

Move a highly perishable product 8,000 miles while beating giants like Costco on both freshness and cost. The mission: execute a global cold-chain flow for 11,558,644 units of aromatic Thai coconut water, maximizing 2026 margins by capitalizing on the 3.20 THB/unit Thai price floor while managing high-cost U.S. West Coast and NYC ports.

U.S. demand analysis

  • Modeled the target market as 2% of total beverage volume across 1,200 stores (Costco, Trader Joe's, Whole Foods).
  • Costco was the dominant channel at 6.38M units, ahead of Whole Foods (2.83M) and Trader Joe's (2.35M).
  • The South and West regions drove the highest regional demand.
  • Shortlisted 7 warehouse candidates to guarantee geographic coverage of the 4 major port regions while allowing clustering efficiency.

Network & port strategy

  • Clustered 1,200 stores into 7 distribution centers — DC4 emerged as the primary Northeast hub (363 stores, 3.5M units).
  • Mapped store clusters geographically in Tableau, isolating DC6 (Hawaii) and DC7 (Alaska) as remote, low-demand outliers.
  • Evaluated four U.S. ports — Los Angeles, Houston, Savannah, and New York City — against distance to each DC.

Sourcing from Thailand

  • Built a weighted supplier scorecard across 8 KPIs: annual capacity, yield, harvest/month, distance to plant, lead time, damage rate, sensory quality, and cost/unit.
  • Selected the 5 most reliable farms to feed the Laem Chabang processing facility — total capacity 13.4M units against 11.56M demand (a 1.86M-unit surplus buffer).

Scenario analysis & optimal flow

  • Stress-tested demand at 2%, 3%, and 4%: 5 suppliers meet 2%, 8 meet 3%, while 4% needs 20 suppliers and still leaves a 2.98M-unit deficit.
  • Identified 4% as the network "sweet spot" — ~35 containers/month flow smoothly through the 4 ports without dock bottlenecks.
  • Recommended consolidating from 7 DCs to 4 if demand stays at 2%, to spread cold-chain fixed costs efficiently.
  • Solved the port-to-DC assignment as an optimization model, minimizing total distance × units shipped while meeting all DC demand.

By the numbers

  • 11.56M units / year
  • 1,200 retail stores
  • 7 distribution centers
  • 4 ports: LA · Houston · Savannah · NYC
  • 5 Thai farms selected
